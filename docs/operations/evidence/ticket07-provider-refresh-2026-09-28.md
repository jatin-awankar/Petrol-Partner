## Current free-tier boundaries

- [Supabase Free projects can pause after low activity](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing). [Managed database backups are on paid plans, and database backups exclude Storage objects](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups). The one-hour recovery point therefore needs measured off-site exports for database state and a separate evidence-object recovery plan; a free database account does not supply that proof.
- [Render Free services spin down after inactivity and use an ephemeral filesystem](https://render.com/docs/free). They cannot by themselves run the continuous pilot worker or backup scheduler during published support windows.
- [Cloudflare Workers Free has a 10 ms CPU limit per invocation and five Cron Triggers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/). A small external checker is conceivable, but the existing Node worker cannot be moved there without a separate design and measured execution. [Cron changes can take up to 15 minutes to propagate](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/cron-triggers/); schedule precision and account accessibility need a live test.
- [Better Stack advertises free monitors and heartbeats with three-minute checks](https://betterstack.com/uptime). Its [missed-heartbeat behavior](https://betterstack.com/docs/uptime/cron-and-heartbeat-monitor/) is a plausible independent alert path, but the five-minute operator-receipt target must be measured after stopping the actual worker. Check commercial eligibility and account access before relying on it.
- [Better Stack's pricing page calls its free tier "Free for personal projects" and lists e-mail alerts](https://betterstack.com/pricing). Do not assume the controlled college pilot qualifies or that phone/SMS paging is free; confirm the plan's eligibility and actual notification channels in the account before selecting it.
- [Backblaze B2 includes the first 10 GB free](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing), and [Resend Free lists 3,000 messages per month and 100 per day](https://resend.com/pricing). These allowances do not establish account accessibility, object-lock/read-after-write/key-recovery behavior, SMTP delivery, or zero overage exposure.

## Railway candidate checked after maintainer suggestion

[Railway's Free plan](https://railway.com/pricing) has no subscription charge and can be opened without a credit card. A new account starts with a one-time $5 trial for up to 30 days; after that, [the Free plan provides only $1 of non-rollover usage credit per month](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/free-trial). The post-trial plan limits a service to 0.5 GB RAM and 1 vCPU. The pricing page lists cron jobs as trial-only on Free, and [Free/Trial restart policy cannot be set to Always and permits at most ten on-failure restarts](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/restart-policy). [Railway's own cost guide estimates a continuously running 0.5 GB/0.25 vCPU service at about $10 per month](https://docs.railway.com/guides/cut-idle-costs-serverless); an always-on worker therefore exceeds the $1 free credit before counting API or export work. Serverless sleep cannot preserve one-minute worker processing because the polling worker does not sleep and a sleeping service waits for an incoming wake request.

Railway is a **cardless synthetic-staging candidate**, provided the new account receives full trial network access and stays inside its credit. It is not an evidenced recurring-zero-cost pilot topology. A narrowly supervised operating-window design would require fresh measured memory/CPU usage, manual/supervised startup, hourly export scheduling without post-trial cron, cost headroom, independent paging, and the same recovery drills; no such design has been validated. Do not put real participants or data on a trial whose expiration would stop service.
