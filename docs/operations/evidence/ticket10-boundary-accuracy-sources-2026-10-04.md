Its [API documentation](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api.html) defines the source-update date as repository integration and build date as standardization: neither establishes a 2023 boundary survey or legal update. The metadata supplies no Maharashtra horizontal error statistic, tested tolerance, validation point inventory, or completeness certification.

The [DataMeet state dataset page](https://projects.datameet.org/maps/states/) identifies ECI polling-station scraping and expressly warns of positional shift and other state-name/date issues. It does not quantify or locate that shift. The [States directory](https://github.com/datameet/maps/tree/master/States) exposes shapefile components, not an accuracy report. The separate [ECI folder README](https://github.com/datameet/maps/tree/master/eci) says its pre-2014 constituency files are not properly georeferenced; this is corroborating provenance risk, **not proof that the state artifact uses those exact files or that their shift equals the state's shift**. No reproducible state scrape/calibration or Maharashtra control-point residual report was established. The historical `psleci.nic.in` link could not be retrieved in this inspection; it cannot be treated as a currently accessible validation source.

The authors' [geoBoundaries methodology paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0231866) describes manual review, WGS-84 standardization, metadata validation and topology repair, including zero-buffer operations. Its source corroboration and valid topology are quality controls, not a Maharashtra-specific positional accuracy test or guarantee that all small islands/exclusions are present. This 2020 paper also cannot attest to every transformation in the 2023 pinned build. Agreement among providers may reflect shared source lineage.

The openly accessible [DataMeet issue 35](https://github.com/datameet/maps/issues/35) discusses missing chronology and boundary provenance organization; it is a contributor discussion, not a calibration report. Full GitHub issue retrieval failed in this tool environment, so this investigation does not claim an exhaustive issue audit or prove that no unpublished report exists.

Such maps can help identify names and broad administrative interpretation; their scale is not a certified metre error bound and is insufficient to resolve tiny polygon rings by itself. Existing locally inspected SOI geometry can diagnose disagreement, but its permission and accuracy questions remain separate. Do not copy its coordinates into a replacement without approved reuse rights.

The Maharashtra government's [MRSAC GVMP description](https://mrsac.gov.in/MRSAC/flipbook/project/geo-referencing-of-village-map-project-gvmp) describes a statewide village database referenced to 5.8 m IRS PAN imagery. The advertised pixel size and source map scale are **not measured positional accuracy**. No accessible Maharashtra-wide independent control residuals, complete offshore/island inventory tied to the candidate, or explicit reuse grant for rebuilding its geometry was established. Village aggregation also needs explicit treatment of non-revenue land and water; it is not automatically the state's administrative perimeter.

These sources may help resolve the **identity** of a discrepant island, water feature or enclave, but cannot currently certify the candidate's position or absence of all other omitted exclusions. Satellite imagery identifies coastlines/land cover, not necessarily jurisdiction; visually matching it would not settle administrative meaning.

## Defensible band: what can and cannot be concluded

There is currently **no defensible numeric band to configure**. An observed maximum inter-dataset difference is a diagnostic maximum between those files, not a bound on their errors relative to the intended administrative boundary. More vertices, coordinate decimals, a 30 m routing snap limit, a map scale, or the previously tried 10/100/1,000 m sensitivity values cannot supply the missing evidence.

As a methodological reference, the [FGDC NSSDA](https://www.fgdc.gov/standards/projects/accuracy/part3/chapter3/at_download/file) tests identical well-defined points against an independent higher-accuracy source and reports ground-unit accuracy, geographic distribution and confidence. It reports a 95% statistic, **not a worst-case guarantee**. The [FGDC accuracy metadata guidance](https://www.fgdc.gov/csdgmgraphical/dataq/posaccfq.htm) additionally distinguishes calibrated error propagation from comparison plots and requires temporal effects to be considered. These are useful assessment methods, not Indian legal authority or an automatic substitute for the approved contract. Choosing a probabilistic residual-risk threshold would itself need a product decision; do not silently equate 95% with strict inside-state eligibility.

For a defensible conservative rule, first obtain validated geometry-error coverage for every relevant boundary section, including detached parts and exclusions, plus an explicit bound for coordinate transformation/numerical processing and any endpoint-location uncertainty. Then reject every requested and confirmed endpoint that is outside, on a boundary, within the combined documented bound, or in any unresolved semantic/exclusion region. Do not snap an ambiguous point across a boundary. Unknown error must fail closed; it is not zero. A numerical band is meaningful only over sections and epochs actually covered by the evidence. The same uncertainty must conservatively affect outside-transit classification and provider-edge totals, not just endpoint checks.

## Why geometry omissions require a separate completeness decision

The prior local evaluation found 3 candidate components/0 holes versus 93 SOI components/2 holes. Counts alone do not establish 90 missing islands: parts may be joined differently, displaced, or use different water conventions. The coordinating analysis records exact diagnostic locations and overlap relationships separately.

If a real Maharashtra island is entirely absent, erosion/exclusion around existing candidate edges cannot restore it; eligible endpoints there would remain rejected. Conversely, an absent excluded enclave or genuine administrative hole may leave an endpoint deep inside the candidate eligible, arbitrarily far from every represented edge. A band on those represented edges cannot protect that omission. If a supposed hole is an inland lake still within Maharashtra, treating it as outside may be a dataset convention rather than jurisdiction. Each discrepancy therefore needs identity, jurisdiction and representation evidence; neither zero holes nor SOI's two holes is proof of correctness. Island/road connectivity also does not establish permission for a route or endpoint.

## Evidence needed and pass/fail conditions

1. **Position and epoch:** obtain an artifact-specific source accuracy report, or an independently validated reference with known better accuracy, appropriate date, matching administrative meaning, and reproducible checkpoints/section coverage. Document datum, transformation and uncertainties. **Pass:** an evidence-derived uncertainty treatment covers every accepted part and relevant route segment under the existing conservative contract. **Fail today:** no such report or reference error bound was established. A sample RMSE alone does not bound untested coastline or missing topology.
2. **Completeness and meaning:** reconcile every observed component/hole discrepancy with a dated official administrative description/map or other independently justified jurisdiction record; inventory all detached components and exclusions, including non-revenue areas/water, rather than checking only familiar islands. **Pass:** all discrepancies are explained, all necessary geometry is included through rights-cleared reproducible derivation, and unresolved areas are not silently accepted. **Fail today:** diagnostic differences have no complete semantic inventory. A candidate-only band cannot repair missing parts or exclusions.
3. **Rights and reproducibility:** evidence used only for comparison must remain distinguishable from geometry copied into the product. **Pass:** all adopted corrections have commercial transformation/storage/redistribution rights, source pins, checksums, provenance and transformation records. **Fail:** a correction depends on copying the pending SOI source without permission or another unverified licence.
4. **Actual-artifact acceptance:** only after the first three pass, finish the approved source amendment, boundary integration and authenticated HTTP/PostgreSQL checks, including ambiguity, islands/holes, route-outside upper bounds and failure cases. **Pass:** ticket 10's existing criteria pass with the pinned artifact. Synthetic checks cannot substitute for the preceding evidence.

The decision now needed is whether to **retain the current statewide accuracy/completeness promise and wait for defensible evidence**, or separately authorize a concrete change to that promise (for example, a narrower explicitly validated service area and its excluded regions, or an expressly defined probabilistic accuracy contract). Neither alternative scope nor residual-risk threshold is approved here. No arbitrary distance or hand-drawn exclusion is proposed. Retaining the requirements means no further production implementation is justified by today's accessible evidence. Evidence acquisition can resume when a suitable report/reference is available; contacting providers, purchasing data or commissioning survey work is outside this authorization.

- Near longitude 77.28537, latitude 18.46550: the [Census 2011 Bidar village catalogue](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/6749) assigns Muthkhed (Chondi Mukhed), village code 599974, to Karnataka's Aurad subdistrict.
