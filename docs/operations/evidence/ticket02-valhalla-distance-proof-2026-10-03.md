# Ticket 02 — Valhalla distance progression proof (2026-10-03)

**Result: the candidate edge-based distance calculation worked for three local route directions/destinations across three costing modes and its synthetic database-backed quote tests. It is not a production routing integration or provider approval.** Real bookings remain disabled, and ticket 02 remains claimed.

The proof used the official Valhalla scripted container (3.9.0) with the public Geofabrik Western India OpenStreetMap extract, on localhost only. It used public coordinates near Amravati and no student, booking, or deployed data. For each of `auto`, `motorcycle`, and `motor_scooter`, `/route` returned a polyline6 route and `/trace_attributes` with `shape_match=edge_walk` returned the same shape and ordered edges. The candidate [normalizer](../../../apps/api/src/modules/posted-routes/valhalla-distance.ts) decoded the shape, checked contiguous edge shape indices, reconciled summed edge lengths with the route summary, and allocated each edge's rounded integer metres across its shape segments using geometric-chord proportions and a deterministic largest-remainder rule. It stores the resulting cumulative integer metres with each shape point. It rejects missing, noncontiguous, or geometrically contradictory data. It deliberately does **not** sum the shorter `shape_attributes.length` values.

The first, approximately 14 km route yielded 183 shape points, 81 edges and **14,062 m** from edge lengths, versus the rounded `/route` summary of **14,064 m** for all modes. A repeated local request produced the same aggregate result. The reverse route produced 14,073 versus 14,076 m for `auto` and `motorcycle`, and 14,264 versus 14,268 m on a different `motor_scooter` route. A longer route produced 31,812 versus 31,818 m for `auto` and `motorcycle`, and 28,701 versus 28,708 m for `motor_scooter`. The first draft's fixed 5 m tolerance rejected the longer routes. A new test reproduced this failure; the revised bound allows at most half a metre per reported edge plus one metre, capped at **20 m absolute**. The observed largest difference between an edge's reported length and the sum of its shape chords was 3.6 m, so the guard now allows at most the greater of 5 m or 2% of that edge's length. All nine route/mode responses passed these revised guards. Responses beyond them fail closed. These are provisional precision rules, not validation of every possible shape or local motorcycle restriction. [Valhalla documents polyline6 precision](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/decoding/) and [trace attributes](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/map-matching/api-reference/).

Two authenticated HTTP/PostgreSQL tests exercised the existing test-only routing seam. A synthetic Valhalla response with an edge-based 1,040 m passenger segment yielded **728 paise** at the car rate of 700 paise/km; the intentionally shorter per-shape lengths did not affect the quote. A response claiming 1,005 m over about 1 m of geometry was rejected as `ROUTING_UNAVAILABLE` and created no posted route. Three focused normalizer tests cover an accumulated edge-rounding difference within the precision bound, a larger summary discrepancy, and an edge length that adds tens of metres beyond its saved geometry. These tests prove the saved-distance quote path and fail-closed behavior for those cases, not a production Valhalla adapter.

**Endpoint snapping is an integration blocker.** On the first route the routed first point was about 48.6 m from the requested origin and the last about 37.0 m from the requested destination; the longer route's last point was about 228.9 m away. The current posted-route validator requires exact endpoint coordinates, so feeding these real results into it would fail. A production adapter must define and test requested-versus-snapped endpoint treatment, safe stopping-place agreement, and the 30 m passenger matching rule. The current normalizer is not wired into production and cannot enable bookings.

Before approving this provider, establish the route-output retention and attribution treatment for this OSM-based use, prove local two-wheeler restrictions and safe stopping, test longer and ambiguous routes and edge-boundary rounding, implement failure/restart behavior, and demonstrate a reliable ₹0 host with monitoring and backups. The lack of an approved production host and the separate ticket 03 external/rate review still block real bookings.

**Validation:** `npm run check` passed against a disposable local PostgreSQL database: API 359 passed/1 skipped, worker 19 passed, plus lint, typechecking, and all builds. Lint reported 9 existing warnings and no errors. The nine live route/mode probes passed the revised normalizer guards. The disposable Valhalla and PostgreSQL containers and temporary graph were removed afterward. No production data or deployment was changed.
